## Limitations

- **H9 did not exercise an outage longer than 120 s.** The longest real outage was 109.9 s (#7). The ESP-NETIF lost-IP path was not triggered, and recovery after more than 120 s without link remains untested.
- **H9 did not reliably measure the time from physical reconnection.** The script times from the operator's Enter, and the serial log records only when the firmware detected the link (polled every 2 s), not when the cable was plugged in.
- **Cycle 1's recovery time is NOT VERIFIED.** The link was restored about 175 s before the next TCP activity. The exact times of the physical reconnection and of the operator's Enter cannot be confirmed from the log alone.
- **The echo is NOT VERIFIED by the log.** The UART loopback is not logged. The echo was checked only by the script.
- **Four cycles had more than one physical outage.** Cycles 1, 2, 3 and 5 each contained two, where the procedure expects one per cycle. The firmware recovered from all of them, but the script's per-cycle "outage" values do not describe them.

## Test Environment

- **Device:** ESP32-Ethernet-Kit, running the firmware above. Before the test, the flashed app was checked against the build (`esptool verify-flash`, digest matched), and the full ELF SHA256 was read back from the app header in flash.
- **Network:** static IPv4 `192.168.1.200`, TCP server on port `4001`. The test PC connected from `192.168.1.58`.
- **UART:** GPIO32 to GPIO33 loopback jumper, so data sent by a TCP client is echoed back.
- **Serial monitor:** `idf.py -p /dev/ttyUSB1 monitor --timestamps`, logged to `fr9-h9-test.log` (now [2026-11-05_monitor-session_fwce94f6077.log](../evidence/2026-11-05_monitor-session_fwce94f6077.log)).
- **Script:** `npm run manual:h9` (`tests/manual/link-recovery.ts h9`), with the default 5 cycles and outages of at least 10 s.
- **Firmware configuration:** `CONFIG_ESP_NETIF_LOST_IP_TIMER_ENABLE=y`, `CONFIG_ESP_NETIF_IP_LOST_TIMER_INTERVAL=120`.

## Procedure

As described in [manual-test-procedures.md](manual-test-procedures.md#h9-repeated-link-recovery-fr-9). For each cycle, the script:

1. connected a TCP client and checked the echo;
2. asked the operator to pull the Ethernet cable at the ESP32, and waited at least 10 s;
3. asked the operator to press Enter and **then** reconnect the cable, and started timing at Enter;
4. probed every 200 ms, in parallel, for a first `ping` reply and for a TCP client the bridge keeps open for 250 ms;
5. checked the echo on the new client, then closed both clients.

The serial log was then reviewed in full. Cycles were matched to the log only through their TCP sessions. Each cycle begins with a "before" client, which an outage closes, and ends with an "after" client closed by the PC (`disconnected by peer`), followed at once by the next cycle's "before" client.

## Serial-Log Evidence: Link Outages

The serial log shows **9** `Ethernet link down (#n)` events and **9** matching `Ethernet link restored ... (#n)` events. The only `Ethernet link up` without a `restored` line is the one at boot, which is correctly not counted as a recovery.

Times are given as ESP32 uptime, then PC time (1 s resolution). Every link event falls on a `…088` ms uptime and every outage is a multiple of 2 s, consistent with the driver's 2 s link polling. Each physical unplug or plug therefore happened up to about 2 s before it was logged.

### Static IP Restoration

After each of the 9 outages, the log shows the same three lines, followed by the matching `(#n)` (outages #1 to #9):

```text
I esp_netif_handlers: eth ip: 192.168.1.200, mask: 255.255.255.0, gw: 192.168.1.1
I NETWORK: IPv4 ready: 192.168.1.200, mask 255.255.255.0, gateway 192.168.1.1
I NETWORK: Static IP restored after link recovery (#n)
```

The log has no `IPv4 does not match the static configuration` and no `static configuration was not kept`.

### TCP Session Cleanup

- Each of the 5 outages that cut an open session (#1, #3, #5, #7 and #8) logged `TCP client receive failed: errno 113`, `TCP client closed: socket error` and `Heap after session`. `disconnect requested` was never logged.
- The 4 outages with no session open (#2, #4, #6 and #9) correctly logged no BRIDGE lines.
- Free heap after each session stayed between 254 284 and 254 620 bytes, and the minimum ever stayed at 250 496 bytes throughout: no sign of a leak.

### Lost-IP Event: N/A

- No lost-IP event appears in the log, with or without `Static IP ... kept`.
- None was required, since the longest outage was 109.9 s.
- A replay of the ESP-NETIF timer behaviour (it starts at a link down, is not cancelled when the link returns, and is not restarted while running) shows that it never expired while the link was down. The closest case was #7: the timer expired at about 1297 s uptime, 10 s after the link returned. The absence of the event is consistent with expectations. This is an analysis of the ESP-IDF v6.1 source, not direct evidence.
- **The lost-IP path was not exercised by this test.**

### Resets and Errors

- **One boot only**, at 09:27:36 (`rst:0x1 (POWERON_RESET)`), almost 2 minutes before the test's first client (09:29:23). This was most likely the reset done by opening the monitor.
- **No reset during the test:** the uptime increases in every line up to 3 640 368 ms, with no step back, and the `LOSS` reports arrive exactly every 10 000 ms with no gap.
- **No crash or error lines:** no `Guru Meditation`, panic, abort, watchdog, brownout or backtrace, and no error-level (`E`) line.
- **Only expected warnings (`W`):** link down, `TCP client receive failed: errno 113`, and the boot warning about uncommitted changes.

### Data Loss: 364 LOSS Reports at Zero

- The log contains **364** periodic `LOSS` reports, from 09:27:47 to 10:28:17. That covers the whole test (09:29:23 to 09:50:31) and the periods before and after it.
- **Every report says `no data loss`, and every counter is 0:** `uart_rx_overflow`, `uart_rx_errors`, `tcp_send_failures`, `tcp_tx_bytes`, `uart_tx_bytes`, `session_bytes`, `no_client_bytes`, `rx_buffer_full`, `uart_breaks`, `event_queue_drops` and `rx_error_pauses`.
- `tcp_tx_bytes`, `uart_tx_bytes` and `session_bytes` count bytes still pending when a session ends (`bridge.c`, `record_session_end`), not traffic. Their zero values mean that no session, whether cut by an outage or closed by the PC, left data undelivered.
- These counters cover only what the firmware can detect. The integrity of the echoed data was checked by the script.

### Cycle 1 Recovery Time: NOT VERIFIED

The link was restored at 447.088 s (09:35:04, outage #2). The log shows no TCP activity until 621.968 s (09:37:59), about 175 s later. The serial log does not record the operator's Enter, so it cannot show when the script's timing began.

Combined with the script's figure of 0.3 s, which is an inference and not log evidence, the Enter was pressed around 09:37:58. The cable had then been connected for almost 3 minutes. The 0.3 s value does not measure recovery from reconnection.

### Echo: NOT VERIFIED in the Log

The script recorded the echo as PASS before and after every cycle. The UART loopback data is not logged, so the serial log cannot confirm it.

## Conclusion

**Verified by the serial log:**

- The bridge recovered from **9** consecutive Ethernet outages of 4.0 s to 109.9 s, without a reset.
- After each one, it restored the static IP `192.168.1.200`, with mask `255.255.255.0` and gateway `192.168.1.1`.
- Every TCP session cut by an outage was cleaned up.
- The TCP server accepted new clients after every cycle.
- The UART loss counters stayed at zero across all 364 reports.

**Measured by the script:** 5 of 5 cycles passed, with the echo working before and after every outage. In cycles 2 to 5, a client was accepted 4.0 s to 6.8 s after Enter, within the 10 s project criterion. Cycle 1's time is not valid.

**Not covered by this run:**

- A physical outage longer than 120 s, and with it the ESP-NETIF lost-IP path (**N/A** here).
- A reliable measurement of the time from physical reconnection.
- Verification of the echo through the serial log.

To cover the first two points, H9 would need another run with `H9_OUTAGE_S=130`, with Enter pressed immediately before reconnecting the cable and only one unplug per cycle.
